# AI Act Transparency

Article 50 of the EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) has applied since **2 August 2026**. It is the transparency chapter: it does not ask whether your system is high-risk, it asks whether people can *tell* that they are dealing with AI.

This skill audits a codebase for that, then closes the gaps.

**You are not giving legal advice.** Say so once, plainly, and then be concretely useful. Obligations attach to roles (provider vs. deployer) and to whether a system is placed on the market at all — an internal demo is not a shipped product. Flag that, do not hide behind it.

## The four triggers

Article 50 fires on four things. Check each against the code.

| Art. | Trigger | What is owed |
|---|---|---|
| 50(1) | System interacts directly with a person | Tell them it is AI, unless obvious to a reasonably informed person |
| 50(2) | System **generates** synthetic audio, image, video or text | Mark the output as artificially generated, in a **machine-readable** format |
| 50(3) | Emotion recognition or biometric categorisation | Inform the exposed people |
| 50(4) | Deepfakes; AI text published to inform the public on matters of public interest | Disclose that it is generated or manipulated |

Note the split: 50(2) binds the **provider** of the generating system, 50(4) binds the **deployer**. If you call ElevenLabs or an LLM API, you are usually the deployer — but the visible disclosure is what users judge you on, and it is cheap.

Also owed regardless: disclosure must be given **at the first interaction**, be clear and distinguishable, and meet accessibility requirements.

## Audit

Find every point where AI output reaches a human. Do not trust a grep for "AI" — the gap is almost always something already shipped and unlabelled.

```bash
# Generation and speech endpoints
grep -rniE 'elevenlabs|openai|anthropic|nebius|text-to-speech|speech-to-text|transcri|whisper|tts\b' src

# Where model output is rendered
grep -rn 'summary\|inference\|extraction\|generated' src --include='*.tsx'

# What is already disclosed
grep -rniE '\bAI\b|artificial|KI-|synthet|automat' src --include='*.tsx'
```

For each hit, answer three questions:

1. **Does a human see or hear this output?** If no, no Article 50 duty (other duties may still apply).
2. **Is there a visible marker at the point of use** — not buried in a footer three screens away?
3. **Is there a machine-readable marker** on generated media?

The classic miss: a synthetic voice. Teams label the chatbot and forget that the friendly announcement voice is generated. Audio is the least-labelled modality and the most convincing one.

## Fix

Four placements, in descending order of how much they matter.

**1. At the generated output itself.** A badge next to the thing, not a policy page.

```tsx
<p className="ai-notice">
  <span className="ai-badge">AI</span>
  Synthetic voice, generated by AI · KI-generierte Stimme
</p>
```

**2. Machine-readable on generated media.** Headers are the cheap honest floor; they are not watermarks — do not claim they are.

```ts
headers: {
  "Content-Type": "audio/mpeg",
  "X-AI-Generated": "true",
  "X-AI-Generated-By": "elevenlabs/eleven_flash_v2_5",
}
```

If the upstream provider applies real provenance marking (C2PA, an audio watermark), say which one and verify it — do not assume it.

**3. Before the user gives input to a model.** Speaking into a microphone is the moment to say where the audio goes.

```tsx
<p className="ai-notice">
  <span className="ai-badge">AI</span>
  Your recording is transcribed by AI · KI-Transkription
</p>
```

**4. One central disclosure.** Name which AI does what, and name the human check.

> **AI transparency · KI-Hinweis.** This prototype uses AI to transcribe speech,
> to summarise what you said, and to read announcements in a synthetic voice —
> you are not listening to, or speaking with, a person. Every AI result is shown
> for review and is never applied until someone confirms it.

## Design rules

- **Label at the point of use.** A footer disclosure does not cover a voice playing three screens up.
- **Say what it does, not that it exists.** "Summarised by AI — check before you confirm" beats "Powered by AI".
- **Separate reported from inferred.** If a model draws conclusions about a person, show what they actually said next to what the model concluded, with confidence. This is the strongest pattern available and it costs one extra field.
- **Name the human checkpoint.** "Never applied until someone confirms it" is both a transparency statement and an oversight claim — only write it if it is true.
- **Match the content's languages.** If the product speaks German, the badge is bilingual. A compact `EN · DE` line beats an i18n framework for a prototype.
- **Do not over-claim.** "GDPR compliant", "fully compliant", "certified" — never write these. Describe behaviour.

## Verify

Do not report this as done from the diff alone.

```bash
# Machine-readable marker actually ships
curl -sI -X POST "$URL/api/announcements" -d '…' | grep -i x-ai-generated

# Visible notice reaches the DOM (expand client-side state first)
# then read the a11y snapshot, not just the HTML source
```

Confirm each visible notice renders where the output appears, in the real deployment. State plainly what you could not verify.

## Beyond Article 50

If the audit surfaces these, name them once and stop — they are different regimes, not this skill's job:

- **Sending user audio or text to a third-party API** is a GDPR question (legal basis, processor agreement, transfers), not an AI Act one.
- **AI literacy (Art. 4)** has applied since 2 February 2025 and covers staff operating the system.
- **Prohibited practices (Art. 5)** — emotion inference in workplaces or education, social scoring, untargeted face scraping. If you find one, that outranks everything above.
