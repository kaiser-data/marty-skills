## 2026-08-16 — origin — `HELPED`

Written from a session on `do_models_have_minds`, a study that reruns a
published preference-coherence metric against a control arm. Every rule in the
skill is a failure that session hit, most of them caught only because something
forced a check.

- **Two scales, no bridge.** Real and invented outcome utilities came from
  separate Thurstonian fits and had been treated as comparable for weeks. They
  are not. A MIXED arm — one real option against one invented one in the same
  comparison — was the only thing that could locate one on the other, and it
  produced the study's calibration point.
- **Collapse.** Two outcomes differing only by a dollar amount rendered as the
  *same sentence* under the magnitude-removing control. 61 adjacent pairs do
  this. It first surfaced as a public web page showing two identical options as
  an example of a contrast.
- **Leakage.** Schwartz defines Power partly as "control … over people and
  resources"; `control` appears in 24% of the power items and 0% of everything
  else. Substituting `dominance` (0 of 510 items) kept fidelity to the source
  and removed a guaranteed false positive.
- **Coverage.** Mapping ten values onto the battery: Conformity had **zero**
  items, Tradition eight. Found before any persona text was written; running
  them would have cost ~1,600 GPU-min for uninterpretable nulls.
- **Trivial compliance.** A directive-following gate thresholded on final P(A).
  One model passed having started on the commanded side — the instruction was
  credited with an effect it did not cause. Fixed by requiring movement from the
  model's own baseline; the bug then recurred one level down in the direction
  control and had to be fixed twice.
- **Strength vs direction.** The study's headline: coherence stays ~flat while
  decisive preferences collapse ~17× per model, because the metric thresholds to
  a hard label.

**Not baseline-tested per superpowers:writing-skills TDD protocol** — the
operator had standing instructions against spawning subagents. Grounded in
observed failures instead. Should get pressure-scenario testing before it is
relied on as a discipline skill.
