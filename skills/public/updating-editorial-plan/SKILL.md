# Updating an Editorial Plan

## Overview

A content calendar fails in one specific way: it fills with dates for posts that do not exist yet. Every slot then arrives with something missing, the backlog reschedules, and the plan reads as a list of failures instead of a plan.

**Core principle: a slot belongs only to a text that is finished. Everything else gets a work deadline and a queue position.**

Publishing capacity is never the constraint. Finishing is.

## Start With The Only Number That Matters

Before touching a single row:

```
published (with a live URL)  vs  open items that already have a text
```

That ratio is the diagnosis.

| Ratio | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| open ≈ published | Healthy | Normal slot planning |
| open 2–3× published | Backlog forming | Stop adding slots; work the queue |
| open > 5× published | The calendar is a wishlist | **Restructure** — strip dates from everything unfinished |

Do not add a single new slot while the ratio is bad. New slots in a backlogged plan don't produce posts; they produce more overdue rows.

## Channels Have Independent Capacity

Count limits **per channel**, never across them. A personal feed, an organisation page and a messenger group reach different people at different moments — they do not compete for the same attention.

| Channel type | Typical limit | Governed by |
|---|---|---|
| Personal feed | 2–3 long-form/week, never two a day | Audience fatigue |
| Organisation page | Tied to events, not a weekly quota | Whether there's something real to say |
| Messenger / member group | Only when it's useful to the member | Utility — a reminder, a venue, a time |

Consequence when triaging: a backlog on the personal feed is not eased by moving posts to a page, and a quiet page is not a reason to publish. Check whether the backlog is one channel's problem before restructuring all three.

## Ranking The Queue

Sort by these, in order. Stop at the first that decides:

1. **Time-bound beats timeless.** An event has a deadline; a lesson piece does not. When they collide, the timeless one moves — always, never the reverse.
2. **Finished beats better.** A publishable good post outranks an excellent blocked one. The blocked post is not a candidate at all until its blocker falls.
3. **Blocker cost decides ties.** One mechanical fix (trim 1,000 characters) beats four approvals from other people. Prefer the blocker you control.
4. **Age, last.** Oldest first — but only among items that already passed 1–3.

## Folding In New Events

A new event is two things at once, and the second is easy to miss:

- **Supply** — material for a post afterwards
- **A deadline** — it makes a *related existing* post time-bound

The second matters more. A dormant post about last month's project becomes time-bound the moment the same organiser runs a new event: publishing just before it rides an audience that is already paying attention.

**Verify every event date against a live source.** Never schedule against a remembered date — event pages move, and a wrong date silently misses the whole window. Check whether "two events" are actually two listings of one event; that mistake is common and changes the plan.

**Reserve one slot per week for fresh material.** A plan with no free capacity forces every new event to displace something, which is how backlogs form. Leave the slot empty at planning time and fill it the evening before. An empty slot filled on the day costs nothing; an overbooked one costs a reschedule every week.

## Before A Slot Is Assigned

A post is only a candidate when all of these are true. Check them before the date, not after:

- **It passes the platform's gates** — length, opening line, tag count, no links in the body
- **Rights are clear** — third-party photos carry attribution conditions; some require visible credit in the post itself, not a comment
- **Named people consented** — a name in a draft is a blocker until the person agrees
- **Claims are sourced** — the strongest claim is the one that survives a hostile reader; check what the material actually establishes versus what it suggests

Anything unresolved keeps the post in the queue. It does not get a date "provisionally".

## Assigning At The Eve, Not In Advance

Set a fixed hand-off time the evening before publication (20:00 works). At that moment, the top ready item takes the next slot.

Two failures become impossible: a post cannot be "late" before it exists, and a finished post cannot sit unpublished because the calendar already promised the day to something else.

A missed hand-off is the warning, not the lost slot — decide that same evening: finish it, or move it deliberately. Never improvise the next morning.

## When A Post Should Not Be Published

Retiring a post is a result, not a failure. Say so explicitly and record why:

- Its occasion has passed and it reads as a recap of something nobody remembers
- It would compete with a fresher post about the same thing
- A consent or rights blocker cannot realistically be resolved before it goes stale
- It has been rescheduled three times — that is data about its priority, not bad luck

Give every blocked item a **hard cutoff date** when it enters the queue. Without one, "later" is indefinite and the row sits there for months.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---|---|
| Scheduling an unwritten post | Creates a deadline for work, disguised as a plan |
| Adding slots to fix a backlog | The bottleneck is finishing, not capacity |
| Moving a time-bound post so a timeless one keeps its day | Inverts the one rule that always holds |
| Letting a finished post wait for a "better" slot | The most expensive error there is — nothing publishes |
| Counting limits across channels | Under-uses the page and the group, over-loads the feed |
| Rescheduling without a cutoff | Turns a decision into a permanent open row |
| Trusting a remembered event date | Misses the window entirely, invisibly |

## Red Flags

- A row whose date moved more than twice
- More items in `blocked` than in `ready`
- A week with every slot filled and nothing published from it
- "We'll finalise it that morning"
- A blocked post holding a prime slot while a finished post has none

**All of these mean: strip the dates, rank the queue, assign at the eve.**
